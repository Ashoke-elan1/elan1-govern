Completeness gaps — built

28 October 2026, night. This report covers every item that the evening check (09-completeness-check) marked Missing or Partial, across L1, L2, L3 and Sovereign. The public site is not included.

How it was built. Twelve builders worked in parallel, one per area. Then came a type-check and production build, an independent check of each area against its item list, a fix round for every gap that check found, and a final build. After that, the app was crawled live in the browser at every level.

3. What is still partial

ItemWhat is missing
Pilot tier shown in a demo organisationThe pilot is fully built for organisations created by sign-up. That covers the ₹0 line, the hard stop at 2,000 actions (TENANT_STOPPED, with no record written), the 30-day read-only period and the move to the paid plan. Demo Traders shows its pilot history and has End the pilot now. The pilot invoice line and the Today tile still appear only in a sign-up organisation. Seeding them into a main demo organisation would freeze writes that every other journey uses.
Seeded demo events carry the seed timeSeeded path-to-production, records and pack events are stamped when the seed runs, not back-dated. Back-dating would make timestamps run backwards in the hash chain. Weaving them into the generated history is a larger change to the chain generator.

7. All choices the builders made

The builders' own notes, kept for review. Section 4 lists the ones that most need a decision.

Sign-in and sessions

  • SAML 'Require SAML for everyone' applies to the organisation's own addresses: its org domain or any domain listed for OIDC. External auditors such as Kavita at desaico.in are not in the org's identity provider and keep password plus two-step sign-in.
  • Break-glass exception, from the Sso.tsx help text 'Keep a break-glass owner with a password and two-step sign-in': an active owner with two-step sign-in may use a password while SAML is required. The two-step code is then mandatory and auth.signed_in records break_glass=true. Dual control for break-glass was left to the security area.
  • The SAML seed was changed to not required at L2, L3 and Sovereign, so all demo password accounts keep working until an owner switches it on. Older saved state that has enforced=true with no identity.updated receipt saying so is quietly set back to not required, because that flag never had any effect before.
  • The seeded suspension is applied when Northbank's records first open, not baked into db.ts. It writes a real member.suspended receipt by Neha Bhatia dated at that time, and it also covers browsers with saved state from up to 12 hours earlier.
  • Portal receipts use actor 'system', because portal people are not console users; their name and employer are in the receipt details. Portal sessions last 12 hours.
  • The sign-in policy lives in a new shared module, src/features/auth/signInPolicy.ts, used by /login, /approve/:token and registry.checkPassword. checkPassword now checks the password itself instead of calling api.signIn, so a correct password for a suspended account no longer counts as a failed attempt.
  • The OIDC SSO-only check still runs before the password is checked, with no refusal receipt, as before. Refusals after a correct password write auth.sign_in_refused.

Approvals

  • New state lives on the org record as an optional approvalsExt field (src/mock/approvalsExt.ts), defaulted on read so older saved localStorage state still loads; nothing was added to types.ts.
  • Same-actor default: 'flag' at L1 (Demo Traders) and 'forbid' at L2 and above (Northbank, Kaveri, Deccan). Owners and admins can change it.
  • 'Same actor' means the approver owns the agent that proposed the call. Forbid blocks approving only; rejecting is still allowed.
  • Step-up default: amounts at or above ₹5,00,000, plus every irreversible call. In the seeded queue the ₹6,12,000 payment and the emails need step-up, while the ₹2,40,000 payment only asks when the sign-in is over 10 minutes old.
  • Blast radius classes: external transfers or EXT accounts are 'cross-boundary'; production, access-changing or bulk tools are 'whole organisation' (tenant); money, external sends or a counterparty make 'one account'; everything else is 'single record'. Tenant and cross-boundary trigger two-person approval at every level, so the seeded production deploy now needs two engineers.
  • The approvals outage is per organisation and only a demonstration: owners and admins trigger it from /approvals/settings, and /status reflects it. Ending it moves every waiting approval's expiry, and unused links', forward by the downtime, which is how 'nothing expires while this is down' is implemented.
  • Reversal window is 30 days from the decision. Reversible: payments, bank-detail changes, vendor creation, deploys, merges and generic writes. Irreversible: external transfers, emails and deletes. Reads have nothing to reverse. Checks are called 'checks' on screen (never 'gate'), with G1–G9 ids and declaration versions.
  • Demo seeding runs once per org, deferred with setTimeout so nothing is written during a render: one live delegation (the procurement approver's role to the EU finance lead's role, 4 days) and one already-reversed approved payment decided 2–20 days ago, each with its own receipt.

Work: stop, break-glass, incidents, telemetry

  • Emergency stop: engaging is one person at every level; lifting needs a second, different owner/admin from L2 up including Sovereign; at L1 one owner/admin lifts. The second person types RESUME and re-enters the password if their sign-in is older than 10 minutes. api.liftStop now refuses at L2+.
  • Break-glass needs two people at every level, L1 included. Access is only for an active owner or admin. The approver must be neither the requester nor the person who gets access. The time box is 30 minutes, 1, 2 or 4 hours, and access expires by itself (breakglass.expired, written by the system). Its effect: password sign-in is allowed while the organisation is set to SSO only or SAML required.
  • Security incidents use kind 'health' with new subkinds key_compromise / isolation_breach / security_other, labelled 'Security · …', so the Incident['kind'] union in types.ts is unchanged.
  • CERT-In: key compromise and isolation breach always start the 6-hour clock from the notice time; other declared classes start it if ticked. Only an owner may record 'not reportable', and never for those two classes. A qualifying incident cannot be resolved until the submission or the 'not reportable' record exists.
  • 'Decided without opening' means the approver did not open /approvals/:id before deciding. Opening is tracked from now on by SystemWatch. For approvals decided before this change, opening is filled in deterministically from a hash of the approval id, with Slack and WhatsApp decisions mostly not opened.
  • The approver telemetry page is for owners and admins only (route gated to /403). Sampling weights fast decisions and decisions made without opening; nobody samples or reviews their own decisions.
  • Pack pilot end = the pack's own pilotEndsAt if another area adds one, otherwise installedAt + 30 days.
  • Maintenance windows are platform-wide, not per organisation, and stored in localStorage key eg:state:ops:maintenance. Owners can start the next planned window early from Today, labelled as a demonstration, and end it from /maintenance.
  • Receipt-write detection in the demo: a write probe to browser storage during 'Run health checks', plus a two-minute write-failure drill. The chain_integrity and receipt_write detectors now run from L1.
  • Seeded history: lifted on read through opsExt on OrgDB, which is optional and defaulted on read. Seeded items carry no receipt numbers because receipts cannot be backdated.

Keys and key compromise

  • The unsigned interval is implemented as an interim Elan1 Govern chain key (SigningKey.interim) that seals records hash-chain-only, so existing verifiers keep passing. The UI labels these records 'not signed with your organisation's key' rather than leaving signatures empty, which would have broken verifiers in several other areas.
  • The compromised key gets status 'revoked' with retiredAt set to the declaration time, so existing key-window checks reject any later use. The key.rotated receipt that stops signing is signed by the interim key, not the compromised one.
  • Chain incident means an active db.incidents entry with subkind 'chain_break' or 'receipt_write'. An open key compromise also blocks ordinary key changes, but not its own bind and signing-on steps. Declaring a compromise is never blocked.
  • Each refused key change is recorded as a key.change_refused receipt before the error is thrown.
  • At L2, turning signing on needs a different owner or admin from whoever bound the key (dual control, like the existing KMS rotation). At L3 and Sovereign, binding needs 3 of 5 key holders.
  • The 24-hour notice goes to a per-org named contact for key notices (seeded as the org's admin who maps to Rahul Menon; editable, and the change is recorded), with an optional copy to the auditor of record. The emails go to the demo outbox via pushMail.
  • Planned successors use a 30-day overlap by default (the IM's 'successor 30 days ahead'), selectable as 7, 14 or 30 days. Seeded L2+ keys get publishedAt up to 30 days before signing; L1 keys get no overlap.
  • Sign-call reconciliation uses IST days over a 14-day window: receipts plus checkpoints signed at L2+, interim-key records excluded. It is shown at L2 (CloudTrail) and with HSM audit-log wording at L3 and Sovereign; L1 gets an upgrade path.
  • DNS TXT format: name _elan1-govern.<domain>, value "v=EG1; kid=<kid>; alg=ed25519; fp=<16 hex>". The printable page is public at /fingerprint/<slug or org id> and shows public keys only.
  • Evidence requests: only auditors (member role 'auditor') request; owners and admins fulfil or decline. Fulfilling runs the real export. A bundle collected after a page reload is regenerated for the same range and recorded as a new export.
  • All new state lives on OrgDB as an optional keysExt, with every field defaulted on read, so saved localStorage state from before this change still loads. Seeds are written without emitting events. The seeded open evidence request's receipt is written only on the first asynchronous read, never during a render.

Evidence, bundles and the verifier

  • The bundle size limit is an estimate of 1,024 KB per bundle (3.1 KB per sealed record, 1.0 KB per L1 record), so the split shows on Northbank's whole chain. Each part is its own bundle, its own evidence.exported receipt and its own ₹2,500 line (billing counts receipts by format, unchanged).
  • The L1 manifest is sealed with the org's current Ed25519 key (manifest_seal, sealing_kid). L1 copy says 'sealed', never 'signed'. Packs made before this are accepted with a note, not failed.
  • Nightly runs are worked out from the chain (02:00 IST; hashes, links, checkpoints since the previous run) and are not stored. Runs on demand also check every signature or seal in the verifier worker, and are stored in OrgDB.evidence.integrityRuns plus an evidence.integrity_run receipt.
  • The unanchored tail is treated as a range problem that is not blocking, with a 'clean range' up to the last anchored seq. Chain breaks and failing pre-flight results block the export until the range is changed.
  • Missing declarations (agent without an AI system, no approved assessment, unsigned rulebook version) block the export until acknowledged. The residency domain comes from api.custody. A transfer basis is needed only when the recipient is outside it, and a purpose only when personal_data records are in range.
  • Sandbox demo data: 4 non-'ask' calls through governCall with environment 'sandbox' for the first active agent that has an sk_sbx_ key, once per org. Billing does not yet exclude sandbox decisions (chainMeter is another area); flag this if sandbox calls should not be billed.
  • The L1 compliance map counts the receipts in the pack against base controls EG-01 to EG-13 (small predicate table in mock/evidence.ts) and maps them to db.frameworks that are switched on. It does not import features/compliance into the mock layer.
  • PASS_L1_INTERNAL applies only to auditor-pack/1 files, or manifests with assurance_levels that are all L1. A bare receipt list does not get it.
  • The manifest field holding the residency, retention and consent answers is named export_checks (not 'gate', which is banned in customer copy).

L1 to L2 upgrade close-out

  • All close-out state comes from receipts in each org's own chain, grouped into an 'episode' that starts at the newest level.upgrade_started for L1→L2. Only the bound key's private seed and the full notice text and address are kept outside the chain, in an optional OrgDB field (upgradeCloseout) that defaults on read.
  • New code lives in new files: src/mock/upgrade.ts, src/mock/upgradeSeed.ts, src/api/upgradeCloseout.ts, src/features/assurance/CloseoutPage.tsx and src/features/assurance/upgrade/{shared,KeyBindStage,NoticesPanel,SignOnConfirm,CloseoutCard}.tsx.
  • The new flow applies only to L1→L2 runs. L1→L3 and Sovereign keep the old KMS stage and confirm screen. If an upgrade happens outside the wizard (for example during onboarding), a new key is created at the switch as before.
  • The switch writes signing_on (seq N) plus nine level.signing_canary records, so the first-ten check has ten signed records at once instead of waiting for traffic.
  • After the switch the key can no longer be replaced; using a different key means cancelling and starting again. Before the switch, a new key may replace an uncompared one, and the replacement is recorded.
  • Switching earlier than the date in the 14-day notice is allowed only with a recorded tick that the named contact agreed. The seeded Northbank close-out uses this path, because its chain window starts only five days before the boundary.
  • Rollback removes the L2 period from levelHistory rather than adding an L2→L1 period. This keeps boundaries, anchoring and seals consistent everywhere else, and means the rolled-back period is billed as L1. The island is recorded in the level.upgrade_rolled_back receipt and listed on the close-out and Assurance levels pages.
  • Rollback is owner only and allowed only before the closing record while the org is still on L2. Running the checks, the first bundle, the closing record and the notices is open to owners and admins.
  • Demo seeding: Northbank (L2) has a complete, closed close-out seeded into its chain; the smoke test showed all seven checks passing live. Demo Traders (L1) runs the whole flow live. Kaveri and Deccan show an explanatory empty state.
  • Notices go to email addresses; receipts store only the mail domain and a SHA-256 digest of the body. Boundary-day and auditor bodies use placeholders that are filled in when the notice is sent.
  • The L1 claim rule is kept: copy about records before the boundary says 'sealed in a hash chain'. 'Signed' is used only for the L2 key and for the island of L2-era records.
  • Verification: I bundled a Node smoke test with esbuild in the scratchpad (no dev server). On Demo Traders it confirmed the bound key signs, signing starts at the named seq, the first ten pass, the bundle exits 0 and the tampered copy exits 1, notices are sent, the closing record is refused while anchoring waits, and rollback works. On Northbank all seven checks pass. The screens have not been checked in a browser.

Records and data protection

  • A 'declaration version' is taken to be a rulebook version, because Seal F07 declaration_version is 'the rulebook version in force'. Withdrawal is an optional `withdrawn` field rather than a new RulebookStatus value, so existing draft and publish logic (status !== 'published') is not affected.
  • Withdrawing the only version in force is refused (publish a replacement or pause the agent first) rather than leaving the agent with no rulebook. Withdrawal needs an owner or admin and a reason; it is not dual-control.
  • Retention mapping: write types containing bill, invoice, payment, journal, ledger, credit_note, voucher, gstin or vendor fall under GST (6 years) and MCA (8 years); transfer, bank_details or ifsc fall under PMLA (5 years). Read verbs (list_, read_, get_, search_) hold nothing. A request is refused only when every matching record is held; otherwise it opens for the erasable records and lists the held ones.
  • Erasure requests from the console now go through api.records.openErasure, which writes the same erasure.requested action with the plan fields added. platform.createErasure is left untouched for any other caller.
  • Most new state lives under db.platform.recordsLedger, created and defaulted on read so older saved state still loads. Model fields go directly on the shared Agent and AiSystem objects as optional fields; withdrawal markers on RulebookVersion.withdrawn. Organisations created by sign-up are not seeded.
  • Demo seeding runs once per org on the first read of any records feature and appends seed receipts at the chain head with the current time; past timestamps are never written. The seed receipts are consent.imported, chain.gap_sealed, erasure.refused, rulebook.version_withdrawn (Northbank, Kaveri) and hosting.dedicated_requested/activated (Northbank).
  • Hosting profile number: Sovereign is 4, L3 is 3 (with an optional dedicated domain), an L2 org with an active dedicated domain is 2, otherwise 1. Only an owner can request, cut over or return; provisioning completes as soon as it is requested, and the owner confirms the cut-over.
  • The consent ledger and G1 are not wired into governCall in db.ts, to keep shared-file edits small. G1 is shown through the tester, the per-consent reliance counts (hashes of the identifiers in decision receipts) and the collect1 pack link.
  • The hosting profile is added to the L2+ evidence bundle manifest only, because profile 2 starts at L2. The L1 auditor pack is unchanged.
  • Who can do what: consent writes, gap records and model edits need an owner or admin. The RBI export is open to every role except approver.

Regulatory packs and profiles

  • The pack-factory state for each organisation lives on db.platform.packFactory, cast the same way features/records/ext.ts does it. Every field is defaulted on read, so platform.ts was not edited and old saved state still loads.
  • Profile orders reuse the existing billing.addon_ordered/addon_cancelled receipts (addon:'profile'), so the meter and invoices bill them unchanged. Seeded orders are written lazily at the head of the chain with today's time, not back-dated, so a seeded organisation's profile line starts this month.
  • Ordering a profile is owner-only (billing.manage), to match BillingPage. Running needs evidence.export (owner, admin or auditor). Issuing needs owner, admin or auditor and is never done by the author. Withdrawing, uninstalling and drills need owner or admin. The G-D answer is owner-only.
  • Screen copy says 'Issued' and 'issuer' for certified and certifier, because 'certified' is a banned word. Reference refusal codes use a CHECK.* prefix, not GATE.*, because 'Gate' is banned in customer copy.
  • Each profile now has its own limits statement, so P10 (unique, over 200 characters) is real. Profile digests changed as a result; bundles exported earlier note that they claim a different version.
  • The fixture bundles for T5–T7 and P3–P6 are built from the organisation's last 120 receipts. The non-conforming bundle is a sound chain with its checkpoints left out, claiming in-mca-audit-trail, so C04 fails. The incomplete bundle drops declarations.json. These only run at L2+.
  • Reconciliation targets follow the demo tools: ledger1 and invoice1 against Zoho Books, collect1 against the Gmail sent log. The ERPNext named in the spec is not in the demo data.
  • The portfolio register is shown per organisation. close1 and gxp1 come from the memorandum's 'earlier portfolio of ten'. The 'general AI governance product' rejection is listed as governance1.
  • Not changed (other areas) — follow-ups for their owners: (1) CompliancePage.tsx:214 and DecisionTrace.tsx:215 show a 'provisional' gate in red as not met; they should treat state !== 'not_met' as passing. (2) BillingPage's own Order/Cancel calls db.logAdmin directly and skips the withdrawn and L2 checks; it could call api.orderProfile/api.cancelProfileOrder instead. (3) chainMeter builds pack lines only from installed packs, so after an uninstall the recomputed past months lose the pack line (the legacy db.ts invoices keep issued months). (4) ExportPackDialog and ExportRangeDialog list profiles that are not ordered; the server now refuses those claims with a clear message, and api.profiles rows now carry entitlement for filtering.

L3: autonomy and counterparties

  • Precedent score formula (precedent-score/1): round(100 × approvals / (approvals + rejections)) − 5 per reversal, clamped to 0–100. Shown as information only and never used for eligibility.
  • Cooldown is 30 days after any revocation of a graduated grant, whether manual or automatic, and blocks re-proposal through graduationCandidates.whyNot.
  • A breach revokes only graduated (A3) grants of the named agent (and tool, if named). The A2 starting level is untouched. Resolving the breach incident does not restore the grant.
  • Who can report a breach: owners, admins and approvers. Declared-by-hand incidents (ops.ts DeclaredClass) do not include an autonomy_breach class, so the Report a breach button and any incident with subkind 'autonomy_breach' are the triggers.
  • Replay rule: the first-arrived copy of an issuer+nonce is kept and later copies fail with RECEIPT_REPLAYED. The customer copy shows the bare code with no 'Gate' prefix.
  • Dual control: owners and admins only, two different people, and only for verified, unreplayed, unrevoked, undisputed copies. A boundSeq is not required, so seeded pending acceptances can be completed.
  • First-issue warning is enforced only through the new *Checked API methods and createDisclosureBundle. The old api.issueToCounterparty and api.createDisclosure are left unenforced so other areas' screens (activity ShareDialogs, records actions) keep working; they should switch to the checked methods and FirstIssueWarning.
  • Revoking an issued receipt also withdraws its linked disclosure package, so the portal link stops.
  • Disputes: receivers in this console raise them and they are mirrored to the issuer. The issuer can record a dispute from an outside counterparty. A signed-in portal user can raise one from the portal. Disputes are resolved on the issuer side (upheld revokes the receipt); the receiver can close its own side.
  • Bundle limits: at most 200 receipts per bundle; expiry 1–365 days (7/30/90 in the UI, or from a profile). Owners, admins and auditors can download; owners and admins can sign and withdraw.
  • All new state lives in an optional platform.l3x (src/mock/l3state.ts) with seed-once flags, so saved browser state from before this change loads and gets the seeds on first read.

Billing and the meter

  • Every commercial term is stored as an admin receipt in the org's chain (billing.* actions); there is no new OrgDB field, so saved state from older builds still loads. Seeding runs once per org from announceOpen, and its receipts carry details.seeded=true.
  • The invoice status 'held' and the credit-note status 'credit' are local to the billing area (ChainInvoiceStatus in chainMeter.ts). The shared Invoice['status'] in types.ts is unchanged.
  • Dispute settlements are recorded in the console by an owner, as elan1's decision with a 'Decided by' field defaulting to 'elan1 billing desk', because there is no elan1-staff console.
  • A discount applies only to governed actions beyond the allowance (the last N committed events of each segment that fall inside the discount's dates). The floor, refusals, packs, profiles, seats and evidence bundles are never discounted.
  • The pilot counts production governed actions (decision receipts, one each, sandbox excluded). The hard stop writes one billing.pilot_stopped receipt. Later refusals with POLICY.TENANT_STOPPED return an unrecorded receipt object (seq 0, rule_id POLICY.TENANT_STOPPED) and are counted in localStorage under eg:data:billing-pilot-stopped:<org>.

  • Cost per governed action is elan1's modelled cost to serve (runtime cost plus 15%) from the financial model: ₹0.092 in Oct 2026 falling straight-line to ₹0.052 from Apr 2030. It is not the customer's effective price.

Status, notices, Sovereign operations

  • No api/index.ts edits: the new calls go through feature-local wrappers (src/features/deployment/sovereignApi.ts and a small noticesApi in NoticesPage.tsx) over new mock modules, following the existing deploymentApi.ts pattern.
  • New per-org state is kept in optional fields on the org DB (OrgDB.sovereign, OrgDB.tenantNotices) by cast, without changing the OrgDB interface. Each is seeded on first read, so state saved before this change still loads and persists with saveState.
  • The internal allow-list is separate from the internet allow-list, which stays empty (deny all). It accepts only .internal/.local/.lan names, the org's own domain, or RFC1918 addresses, and is seeded for Deccan with siem/smtp-relay/itsm.deccanbank.internal.

Only attempts made from the console (webhook to an outside URL) are receipts (egress.blocked); firewall background noise is a log, not receipts. The Deployment 'Blocked in 24 hours' figure now comes from this log instead of platform's random number.
  • Email and phone links stay available in Sovereign (internal mail relay, console over the bank's network); only Slack, WhatsApp and outside webhook URLs are blocked.
  • Rollback moves the bundle running now back to 'staged' (still verified, can be re-applied) and makes the previous applied bundle current, without adding a new status value to the UpdateBundle union.

The post-incident note is shown as sent 20 h after resolution and as 'due by' before then.
